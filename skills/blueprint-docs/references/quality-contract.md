# Quality contract

## Required checks

- Each normative statement has a stable ID, owner, source/rationale, priority, status, and verification method.
- Facts cite evidence. Estimates identify their method and confidence. Assumptions and unknowns remain visible.
- Architecture descriptions identify stakeholders, concerns, viewpoints, model kinds, correspondences, inconsistencies, and rationale.
- Diagrams include a textual inventory of nodes, relationships, trust boundaries, flows, and conclusions.
- Risks distinguish inherent and residual exposure, controls and evidence, appetite/tolerance, response owner, triggers, review date, and acceptance authority.
- Tests trace to requirements, risks, and controls and record environments, data, expected/actual results, limitations, and evidence.
- Releases identify artifact digest, source revision, builder, resolved inputs, provenance, SBOM, verification result, approver, deployment evidence, and rollback artifact when applicable.
- AI-enabled systems add intended/precluded use, provider/model/version as neutral strings, data and knowledge limits, affected groups, human authority and override paths, TEVV evidence, monitoring, incident paths, and accountable approval.
- Accessibility defaults to WCAG 2.2 AA when web content is in scope, with project-specific applicability recorded.

## Claim boundary

Say “aligned with” a standard unless the complete licensed standard, applicability analysis, required evidence, and formal conformance assessment support a stronger claim. Current useful references include ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018, ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022, NIST SSDF 1.1, NIST AI RMF and AI 600-1, WCAG 2.2, W3C PROV-DM, OWASP ASVS 5.0, SLSA 1.2, and NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3.
